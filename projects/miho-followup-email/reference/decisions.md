## 2026-08-07 — Send from our own domain with Reply-To; Gmail OAuth deferred to v2
**Decision:** v1 sends from an authenticated domain we own, with the business name as the From display name and the owner's address as Reply-To. The owner configures nothing — no OAuth consent, no DNS, no Google account required. Gmail OAuth moves to v2.
**Why:** Reverses the 2026-08-07 meeting call that OAuth was the only viable path. That call correctly rejected spoofing the owner's address, but conflated it with display-name-plus-Reply-To sending, which is standard transactional practice and DMARC-aligned because the From domain is ours — Gmail will not stamp it 'via' since the From domain and the signing domain match. Research on the OAuth path: gmail.send is a sensitive scope, not restricted, so no third-party security assessment and no CASA fee — but it still requires Google verification (privacy policy, terms, public homepage, demo video, brand review) before exceeding a 100-user cap that is lifetime-of-project and cannot be reset, and unverified apps show a 'Google hasn't verified this app' warning screen. For the archetype customer — Mike's one-man plumber, a bad presenter with a Yahoo address — that warning is a real drop-off point, and requiring a Google account excludes him outright. Reply-To cuts the MVP to roughly two to three days and removes Google from the critical path entirely. Considered and rejected for v1: sender masking by spoofing the owner's address (gets flagged), and per-user DNS records (far beyond this audience). What OAuth still buys in v2, and the reason it is deferred rather than dropped: the message lands in the owner's own Sent folder and the whole thread lives in their mailbox. The cost accepted for v1 is that a customer who expands the sender sees an unfamiliar domain.
**Supersedes:** OAuth connection over sender masking (2026-08-07 MiHO partner meeting)
